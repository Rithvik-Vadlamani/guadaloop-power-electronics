# Fabrication and supervised prototype testing

This is a design/test plan, not a record of completed manufacturing or testing. Precharge is deliberately deferred. Do not treat the native clean DRC as approval to connect a battery or order an unspecified standard PCB process.

## PCB requirements

- Four layers, nominal 1.60 mm overall, ENIG, outer copper 70 µm and inner copper 35 µm (nominal 2/1/1/2 oz).
- Native dielectric thicknesses: 0.2104 / 0.9392 / 0.2104 mm; solder mask nominal 0.015 mm each face. Confirm the fabricator's realizable equivalent stack before order.
- Added thermal vias: 0.70 mm pad diameter, 0.30 mm finished hole. Coordinates and net assignments are in `Verification/revision-edits.json`.
- All vias intersecting solderable component pads, including inherited U1/Q1 locations and the new Q1/Q2 pad vias, require resin fill, planarization and copper capping (via-in-pad plated over). Tenting alone is not equivalent. Tell the fabricator explicitly; the KiCad file alone does not fully specify the process.
- L1 uses `MV:CODACA_CPRX3231L_150MC`, 5.6 × 10 mm pads with 4.0 × 8.8 mm plated slots. The enlarged slots accommodate the 15 µH variant's lead/spacing tolerances without moving terminal centers. Confirm finished plated-slot tolerances against a real component or current manufacturer drawing before assembly.
- Preserve local libraries and the exact part numbers. R16/C27 stay unpopulated. Test-point and mounting-hole footprints do not represent additional fitted electronic components.
- Have the assembler review QFN/MOSFET paste apertures, copper-capped pad planarity, reflow profile and heavy-copper thermal balance. Inspect solder quality; no visual rendering establishes exposed-pad solder coverage.
- Board-only Gerber/drill/assembly exports are now prepared and reviewed under `Manufacturing/` and `Assembly/`. External input-interface work is deferred separately and does not block preparing this bench-prototype handoff. Fabricator DFM/process acceptance and assembler stencil/orientation approval are still required before an order.

## Cooling fixture selection

For the first test fixture, use two external **Delta AFB0824SHE** 24 V, 80 × 80 × 38 mm fans: one directed over the top-side power stage and inductor, the other across the underside MOSFET copper. Power them from a separate current-limited 24 V supply so cooling runs before the converter starts and remains available if it stops. The manufacturer's listing gives 8.4 W / 0.35 A per fan and 72.76 CFM free-air flow. A 24 V supply rated at least 1 A is a starting requirement; check fan startup demand. Fit guards and secure the fixture without loading the PCB or obstructing either face. These fans are test equipment, not new board-mounted components. [Manufacturer product page](https://www.delta-fan.com/products/AFB0824SHE.html)

Free-air CFM does not establish airflow at the components or a particular heat-transfer coefficient. Tune placement/ducting using temperature measurements. These fans have not been demonstrated to make continuous 20 A operation acceptable. Keep the existing board mounting geometry; provide insulating standoffs and sufficient clear space under the board. Do not attach a common metal heatsink to VIN/SW copper.

## Staged bring-up

High-energy power testing needs suitably rated instruments, guarded connections and an operator experienced with switching converters. A fuse/current-limited supply does not eliminate stored capacitor energy. Keep the battery disconnected until the deferred connection/precharge/fusing work is complete.

1. **Unpowered inspection:** Verify exact values and polarity, L1 terminal fit, connector polarity and MOSFET/controller assembly. Check for shorts and suspect low-resistance paths, interpreting readings in light of body diodes and capacitor charging. Disconnect and verify discharged rails before handling or changing connections.
2. **Bench source only:** Use a slowly ramped current-limited laboratory supply, with leads connected while de-energized. Start unloaded, with the smallest practical current limit; a preliminary 0.1–0.25 A limit is for detecting gross faults, not normal-load testing. Watch for repeated UVLO/restart rather than increasing the current blindly. Initial input sweep may start below UVLO and proceed to 36 V after checks. Do not use a battery hot-plug as a substitute for controlled ramping.
3. **Basic operation:** Verify approximately 24 V output, controller VCC, startup behavior and approximately 100 kHz switching. Establish safe probe connections before applying power. Measure high-side VGS relative to its source, not ground, using suitable differential/isolated probes. Never place a grounded scope clip on SW or a high-side gate. Do not float an ordinary scope's protective earth.
4. **Low-load waveforms:** Begin with a resistive/electronic load near 0.5–1 A. Check both VGS waveforms, dead time, MOSFET VDS overshoot, output ripple and input current. Raise the input toward the verified maximum battery voltage only after acceptable lower-voltage behavior. Stop for parasitic turn-on, cross-conduction, sustained oscillation or unexpected current. A provisional VDS stop threshold is 80 V (a conservative project screening threshold, not a manufacturer rating); if crossed, review ringing/snubber/layout before continuing. Also stay within all other datasheet operating and absolute limits.
5. **Load and thermal steps:** With both fans running, advance approximately 1 → 2 → 5 → 10 A, inspecting waveforms and temperatures at each step. Attempt 15/20 A only after prior steps stabilize with margin. The total output across J3–J6 is 20 A, not 20 A per connector. Leave raw feedthrough J2 unloaded initially. A 20 A test requires a safely rated approximately 480 W load and appropriately rated wiring/connectors/source.
6. **Conservative early stop limits:** Stop at 90 °C measured MOSFET/controller package surface, 100 °C inductor surface, 70 °C electrolytic case, or any rapidly rising temperature, unexpected waveform, smell or discoloration. These are early prototype screening limits chosen below component maxima, not qualified operating ratings; surface temperature does not directly establish junction temperature. Use insulated, securely attached sensors or properly calibrated IR methods. A failed temperature step means improve cooling or revise the heat path before increasing load.
7. **Dynamic checks:** Once static behavior is acceptable, test modest load steps and recovery before larger steps. Record peak voltage/current, settling, input voltage, ambient and fan arrangement. Measure loop response where instrumentation is available. Tune the optional snubber only from measured ringing. Do not begin with deliberate output short-circuits or battery fault tests.

Record results and actual input-source specifications. Continuous operation, enclosure airflow, higher ambient, fault protection, EMC and battery startup remain separate validation items. The current files do not claim those tests have passed.
